PRIVACY · DATA SHEET · REV 2026-11

Privacy

No patient data, by design. Shortfall never asks for, stores or displays patient information. Its only inbound data is public drug shortage data and your site's own content.

What the app reads

  • FDA drug shortages — the public list at api.fda.gov, fetched daily.
  • NLM RxNav — public drug identifiers at rxnav.nlm.nih.gov, used to normalize names and NDCs.
  • Your Confluence pages — page titles, links and owners in the spaces you mark as clinical content.
  • Your Jira work items — summaries, assignees and status of work that names a shorted drug.
  • Teamwork Graph — owner and team relationships for affected content, only where an admin granted the scopes.
  • The drug list you provide — pasted or uploaded by your admin, or the fictional sample hospital.

What the app stores

  • FDA shortage records and their first-seen/last-seen history.
  • Your drug list, the match results, the impact map and response records — all inside your Atlassian site in the app's own Forge storage (Forge SQL and key-value store).
  • Settings chosen during setup and an audit log of sync runs.

What the app sends

Nothing about your site leaves it. The only external hosts the app can reach are the two public drug-data services above, declared in its manifest. No analytics, no tracking, no telemetry to us. This website itself sets no cookies and runs no tracking scripts.

Writes

Creating Jira work items, tasks or page notices happens automatically only if your admin enabled it in settings. Agent-initiated writes always ask for confirmation first.

AI features

Drafting runs through the site's own Rovo agent — there are no third-party LLM calls. The agent writes only from facts the app returns with sources, and a person approves before anything is sent.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app removes its Forge storage on your site. The sample hospital's own remove button deletes exactly what it created.
